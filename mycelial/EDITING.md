# Editing the deck

- **Copy:** The slide sections are in `index.html`, in order from slide 1 through slide 11. The current intro story for slides 4–7 and its scoped responsive typography are in the opening block of `deck-overlay.js`; edit that block for “The solution”, “Meet Astrid”, “Everyone’s to shape and refine” and “How it pays”. It runs synchronously before scene registration, updates the existing semantic headings without replacing them, and suppresses their older fallback body copy. The complete intro remains inside each heading so the existing scene text bounds, masks and transitions include it. Other slides’ main narrative copy remains in `index.html`. Some visuals and labels are generated by the slide script named in the nearby HTML comments.
- **Intro checks:** Run `node --test tests/deck-story.test.mjs` for canonical copy, links, idempotence, paired marker timing, shared explanations, reset/exit cleanup, scroll visibility, reduced motion, overlay interaction and arrival pacing. These dependency-free tests use DOM and clock fixtures; they do not replace real-scene browser or visual checks. Keep scene lifetime and restoration contracts intact when adjusting the intro’s visual hierarchy.
- **Slide 2 field:** Pointer movement inspects dots without steering the camera. Desktop's first automatic highlight beat is silent and cut short by `OPENING_LEAD` (1.5 s), so the first name shown arrives about 2.4 s in; mobile retains its first beat (about 1.25 s in). New labels must have a projected 3.35-second readable window, and measured rectangles reserve an 18–28px gap through the old label's fade. Fading labels stay at their last position. Exit clears highlights before the picture rewinds, and the field cannot introduce new highlights while leaving. Recycling tests each body's own projection rather than the shared previous projection.
- **Slide 5 header:** App and Vision are separate new-tab links under the title; the highlighted Astrid name is not a link. “Meet Astrid” stays on one line above App and Vision. Content-sized columns group the two roadmap lines, brace and “All powered by Open Models” closely; unused horizontal space stays on the right. Slide 5's numbered section label reads “Introducing.” The name glows once after the slide settles. On portrait phones, Today and Soon become equal, centered columns; the desktop brace is replaced by a horizontal, upward-branching connector above the centered “All powered by Open Models” caption. On portrait phones (up to 600 px wide) the order is label, “Meet Astrid”, the demo, then the Today/Soon columns with the connector and “All powered by Open Models” (a slightly smaller footer copy, `.as-mobile-roadmap`), then App | Vision. Both the roadmap and the links are `display: none` clones appended to the slide after `.as` and shown only on phones, while the originals inside the heading are hidden there, so only one visible (and screen-reader-visible) copy exists at each breakpoint; they are `flex: none`, so the demo keeps the flex height between the heading and this block. Tablets (including 600–760 px portrait) keep the roadmap in the heading; their App | Vision links still follow the demo. Reduced-motion preferences disable the glow.
- **Slide 5 copy reveal and scrolling:** The slide 5 intro uses a staggered left-to-right ink sweep on its canonical label, title, roadmap lines, grouping connector and Open Models caption, triggered at the existing network reveal delay (`dot-transitions.js`) and retired with its transition. This leaves direct entry and reduced-motion settings static. Mobile chat retains its entire history and uses vertical touch scrolling (`overflow-y: auto`, `touch-action: pan-y`) instead of hiding all but two messages. The player and timeline keep their own independently sized region.
- **Slide 5 animation and layout:** Do not add a second CSS transition for the intro. The native network reveal/gather in `dot-transitions.js` paints its soft radial edge onto the canonical `.as-copy` text, like slide 4, including the “05 Introducing” label and reverse navigation; the phone-only roadmap and App | Vision links below the demo are outside `.as-copy`, so they follow that same visit's alpha (`--as-reveal-alpha`) instead of the ink sweep. The shared node's tiny idle drift now eases back in after landing instead of jumping to the current sine phase. Timeline clip names, duration chips and the three output markers align to each track's vertical centre. The mobile/desktop demo has slightly more air below “All powered by Open Models”. The first Three.js guide preview begins muted playback as its timeline clip is produced, and the viewer waits for an already-playing frame instead of briefly displaying an unmoving video. The empty composer has no placeholder text.
- **Demo notes and pacing:** `createAstridDemoNotes` in `deck-overlay.js` creates two controls for each of three explanations: one attached to its chat step and one to its timeline output. A new chat step is visible for 500 ms before its marker pulses. The editor marker follows 850 ms after that reveal on the visit clock, never before the output exists. Both delays are cancelled on reset or exit. The image explanation stays at the centre of the reference-image row. Both controls share the same hover, focus and tap explanation and each pulses once per tour. Chat markers track scrolling and hide outside the chat viewport. Reset cancels delayed reveals and animations; reduced motion skips both animation and delay. `demo.js` retains its full-final-film playback and thirty-second final-frame hold. Reading an open note pauses the story countdown. `deckStoryPacing` retains 1.45× forward animated arrivals on slide 7 and 1.3× on slide 8; direct, reduced-motion, reverse and settled visits keep their native pace.
- **Slide 6 layouts:** The stack slide has four arrangements, chosen by the viewport. *Stacked* (a phone, or any upright screen up to 1100 px wide, iPads in portrait included): text on top, the stack under it with short flags down its left, and one card slot below, centred and at most 620 px wide (`CARDW_SLOT`). On a phone (stacked and up to 600 px wide) the card shows the layer's title, its line, the examples' names only (no italic sub-line under each, no Models `.st-more` list) in two columns, and the footer; the slot is as tall as the tallest card, so the shorter card gives the stack above more height. Upright tablets keep the sub-lines. *Snug* (any screen on its side over 500 px tall that is under 1280 px wide or has a touch screen, so every iPad in landscape): side by side as on the desktop, with a 29vw text column in smaller type, a 264 px card (`CARDW_S`) showing the layer's line, three examples and its footer (no fourth example, no `.st-more` line). *Desktop* (on its side, 1280 px or wider, with a mouse): unchanged, a 300 px card with every example. *Low* (on its side, 500 px tall or less: a phone on its side): the 240 px card over the flags. The rules live in three places that must agree: the `STACKED` and `SNUG` media queries at the top of the layout section of `stack.js` (which also fit the stack to the room left), the matching `.st-` media queries in `index.html` (flags and cards), and the `.st-host.story-refined` media queries in `deck-overlay.js` (the heading's column and type). The stack is always scaled to fill whatever room the text and card leave it.
- **Figures and links:** Slides 10–11's initiative data and links are in `flywheel.js`. Slide 8's illustrative people counter and timing are in `flowers.js`.
- **Slide 1 subtitle and its note:** The subtitle is the `.body` under slide 1's headline in `index.html`; its copy is one line (the `&nbsp;` keeps the dash with its phrase). The gold ¹ after "economy" opens `.vc-note`, the card just after slide 1's `.split`: its two bars (in the inline SVG), its line and its source link are edited there. - **Slide 1 arrival preview:** The opening carousel starts preloading the intended front film when the ring begins to arrive. At 55% of its arrival (about 1.2 seconds before landing) it begins muted playback, allowing the video to be visibly moving when the card lands. Landing retains the current video rather than restarting it; the carousel's dwell then uses the preview's remaining duration. Frozen or reduced-motion states still obey existing media rules.
- **Slide 1 recipients:** The seven `.vc-tile` entries are in the slide 1 markup in `index.html`; they stand round the ring in markup order (which is also the Tab order), and `collage.js` lays the ring out for however many there are. Each uses a `<picture>` with an AVIF source and JPEG fallback still, a `data-preview` MP4 that plays while the card is at the front, and `data-film` for its Cloudflare Stream overlay film. Every card is square: `data-square` is the object-position that crops its still and preview to the square, and `data-focus` places the still within its film's 16:9 frame, so the player opens out of the card. The tile's `data-title` and `data-artist` supply its title and artist (the name inside each card's top edge is generated from them); the `data-page` is its external entry link. The ring's label and its Arca Gidan link are in `.vc-label` in the same markup.
- **Link preview image:** `mycelial-og.jpg` is a 1200×630 JPEG captured from slide 1 at 2× and downsampled, with the footer chrome and motion ring hidden. Start the local server with `PORT=8240 python3 serve.py`, open `http://127.0.0.1:8240/mycelial/index.html` in Playwright at a 1200×630 viewport and device scale factor 2, wait for the opening to settle, inject CSS to hide `.chrome` and `#motion`, and save the screenshot. - **Slides 9–11 copy:** The wheel's text uses the same ink reveal as slides 3 and 5 (see [wheel-copy-ink.md](../docs/wheel-copy-ink.md)). Text added there needs an ink colour in the `.fw-host .fw-spreading …` rules in `index.html`, or it will not take part in the reveal; boxes that are not text follow `--fw-reveal-alpha`.
- **Text pace and step-back reveal:** `copyExtra` in `dot-transitions.js` lengthens a forward reveal: slide 2 by 1.5 s (about 2.5 s on screen), slide 4 by 1 s with its start unchanged, slide 7 by 2 s of its 1.45× clock (about 2 s on screen), and slide 8 by 1.3 s of its 1.3× clock (a second more on screen). Slide 8's words wait for slide 7's core to finish crossing (`CROSSED` in `flowers.js`, a text hold released on the scene clock). On any step back the arriving words are not revealed forwards again: they return by the gather run backwards (`unwinding`, `easeUnwind`; `UNWIND` sets its length, three times the gather's: 1.2 s on screen at any pace). Ink selectors for slide 5's name must out-rank its own gradient rule, or the name shows while the rest of the heading is still hidden.
- **Step-back alignment:** On a step back the words land with the picture. `REVERSE_PICTURE` in `dot-transitions.js` holds each reverse picture's measured length on the deck clock (by the slide stepped back from); the unwind starts so that it ends at that landing. Where the picture keeps the words waiting past that point (it still crosses their place), the unwind is squeezed, down to `UNWIND_FLOOR` (0.7 s), and follows as soon as they are clear. If a picture finishes sooner than forecast, the words follow at once. Changing a scene's reverse animation length means updating its entry in that table. `UNWIND_TUNE` beside it holds the step-backs set by eye instead (11→10, 10→9, 9→8, 8→7, 5→4): start and length in ms on screen, and whether the words may start before the picture has cleared their place.
